Money questions people ask

Short, plain answers. Education only.

How much emergency fund should I keep in India?

A common guide is to keep 3 to 12 months of essential expenses: about 3 months if single with a steady job, about 6 if you have a family, and 9 to 12 if your income varies. Many people keep the first month in a savings account and the rest in easy-to-withdraw options.

How can I start investing with Rs 1,000 a month?

Many Indian investors start with a small monthly SIP in a mutual fund. The key habits are to start early, invest regularly and keep costs low. Read the fund's documents first, and consider speaking to a SEBI-registered adviser.

What is a SIP?

A SIP (systematic investment plan) lets you invest a fixed amount in a mutual fund at regular intervals, usually monthly. It spreads your buying over time instead of one large purchase.

What is a good CIBIL score?

A score of 750 or above is generally considered good by lenders. Paying bills and EMIs on time and keeping credit card use low are the usual ways to build it.

What is the 50/30/20 budget rule?

A simple budgeting guide: about 50% of income for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and debt repayment. Treat it as a starting point and adjust it to your own life.

How much can I save under Section 80C?

Under the old tax regime the Section 80C deduction limit has been Rs 1.5 lakh a year. Tax rules change, so check the current rules on the Income Tax Department website or with a tax professional.

What is the difference between CAGR and XIRR?

CAGR shows smoothed yearly growth between two dates for a single lump sum. XIRR accounts for the timing of several cash flows, so it suits SIPs.

What is an expense ratio in a mutual fund?

It is the yearly fee, as a percentage of assets, that a fund charges to run the scheme. A lower ratio leaves more of the return with you.

Which personal finance book should I read first in India?

Many people start with The Psychology of Money for mindset, and Let's Talk Money for an Indian guide to insurance, mutual funds and taxes.
